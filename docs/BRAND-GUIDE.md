## 1. Brand foundation

### 1.1 What the organisation does

Healing Horizons Behavioral Health Services provides **Psychiatric Rehabilitation
Program (PRP)** services to children, adolescents and adults already receiving
behavioral health care. PRP is skills-based rehabilitation, not therapy: it helps
people build daily living skills, independence, coping capacity and community
integration alongside their clinical treatment.

That distinction drives the whole brand. This is not a therapy practice selling a
therapeutic relationship. It is a **rehabilitation and skill-building service**,
and the visual language is correspondingly more active, more optimistic and more
multi-coloured than a counselling brand would be.

### 1.2 Two audiences, not one

This is the single most important strategic fact about the brand, and the
brochure already reflects it:

| Audience | What they need | Where they look |
|---|---|---|
| **Referrers** — hospitals, physicians, therapists, schools, case managers, community organisations | Credibility, service area, scope, how to refer, speed of response | "Who We Serve", "Now Accepting Referrals From", "Service Area", the QR code |
| **Clients and families** — self and family referrals | Warmth, dignity, "will this help me", what happens next | The photograph, the pillars, "Why Choose Us", the quote |

**Design implication:** every substantial piece needs both registers. Referrers
scan for facts; families read for tone. The brochure solves this by splitting
panels — a warm emotional left panel, a factual centre, a reassuring right. Keep
that structure.

### 1.3 Brand attributes

Taken from the four pillars and the taglines, and stated so each one changes a
decision rather than describing a mood:

| Attribute | In practice it means |
|---|---|
| **Hopeful** | Sunrise, ascending forms, warm light. Never clinical grey. Never the visual language of illness. |
| **Dignified** | People are shown as capable, active and in relationship — never as patients, never faceless, never photographed from above. |
| **Active** | Verbs, not conditions. "Build", "strengthen", "achieve" — the brand is about what someone does, not what they have. |
| **Credible** | Real credentials, real service area, real referral paths. The referrer audience will not forgive vagueness. |

### 1.4 The horizon idea

The name gives the brand its central image: **a sunrise over a horizon** — light
arriving, the day ahead, the line you are moving toward.

It appears in three places and should not be stretched further:

- The logo emblem — sun rising over layered landscape.
- The rainbow wave at the foot of every layout.
- The ascending, left-to-right rhythm of the pillar sequence
  (Heal → Empower → Support → Thrive).

### 1.5 What this brand is not

- Not clinical-institutional. No cold blues-and-greys, no stock hospital imagery.
- Not a wellness or lifestyle brand. No mandalas, no lotus flowers, no incense.
- Not childish. The multi-colour palette is optimistic, not playful — it serves
  adults and families as much as children.
- Not a therapy practice. Do not borrow counselling-brand language ("your
  therapeutic journey"); PRP is skills work.

## 2. The logo

### 2.1 Construction

The logo is a **circular emblem above a stacked wordmark**.

**The emblem** contains, from top: a rainbow arc; birds in flight; a rising sun;
a tree with multi-coloured foliage; a human head in profile, right-facing; a
group of stylised human figures; and cupping hands forming the base. It reads
as "growth, mind, community, support" — read outward from the centre.

**The wordmark** is three stacked elements:

1. **HEALING** — large, bold, all caps, with a horizontal colour gradient running
   purple → blue → green.
2. **HORIZONS** — green, all caps, smaller, flanked left and right by short rules.
3. **BEHAVIORAL HEALTH SERVICES** — purple, all caps, small, generously letterspaced.

### 2.2 Clear space and minimum size

- **Clear space:** at least the height of the "H" in HEALING on all four sides.
  Nothing intrudes — no text, no rules, no photo edges.
- **Minimum size, full lockup:** 32mm / 120px wide. Below this
  "BEHAVIORAL HEALTH SERVICES" becomes unreadable.
- **Minimum size, emblem alone:** 12mm / 44px. Use the emblem alone for
  favicons, app icons, social avatars and the QR-code centre.
- **Minimum size, emblem + HEALING HORIZONS only** (drop the descriptor):
  20mm / 76px. This is the preferred small-space lockup.

### 2.3 Approved variants

You need all five produced as vectors:

1. **Primary** — full colour on white.
2. **Reversed** — full colour emblem, white wordmark, for navy or purple grounds.
3. **One-colour purple** — for single-colour print, faxes, stamps.
4. **One-colour white** — for photo overlays and dark grounds.
5. **Emblem only** — square-croppable for avatars and favicons.

### 2.4 Misuse

Do not: recolour the emblem, stretch or condense, rotate, add effects (drop
shadow, bevel, outer glow), place the full-colour version on a mid-tone
background where the wordmark loses contrast, re-typeset the wordmark in a
substitute font, or separate HEALING from HORIZONS.

**The gradient in HEALING is part of the wordmark artwork, not a live text
effect.** Never attempt to recreate it by typing.

## 3. The four pillars

**HEAL · EMPOWER · SUPPORT · THRIVE** is the brand's organising idea. Each has a
fixed colour and icon, and the order never changes — it describes a progression.

| Pillar | Colour | Icon | Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HEAL** | Purple `#5C2D91` | Heart | Stabilise; address what hurts |
| **EMPOWER** | Blue `#1B75BC` | People / figure | Build agency and self-direction |
| **SUPPORT** | Green `#2E9E48` | Supporting figures | Sustain through family, community, staff |
| **THRIVE** | Orange `#F58220` | Figure with raised arms | Live well beyond the service |

### Rules

- **Order is fixed.** Heal, Empower, Support, Thrive. It is a sequence, not a list.
- **Colour-to-pillar mapping is fixed.** Purple is always Heal. Never swap.
- **Use all four or none.** Do not pull out a single pillar as a standalone badge —
  it breaks the progression the brand is built on.
- The pillar row is a **signature device**. Use it on the homepage, the brochure
  cover, and the about page. Repeating it on every page dilutes it.

> **Contrast warning:** the Support green and Thrive orange **fail** contrast
> requirements as text. They are icon-and-badge colours. See [§4.3](#43-the-two-colours-that-will-fail-you).

### 4.3 The two colours that will fail you

> **Thrive Orange `#F58220` is 2.59:1 on white. It fails every WCAG threshold,
> including large text. Support Green `#2E9E48` is 3.44:1 — it fails for body
> copy and fails outright on the lavender panel (2.94:1).**

These are the two colours a designer will most want to type in, because they are
the brightest and most cheerful. They must be used only for:

- icon fills and strokes at 20px+
- badge and chip backgrounds carrying **white or ink text**
- the rainbow wave and other decorative graphics
- chart fills that also carry a text label

## 5. Typography

> Typeface names below are visual identifications from the artwork. Confirm
> against the source files before licensing anything.

| Role | Face | Weights | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wordmark** | Geometric sans — Montserrat Bold or Poppins SemiBold | 600–700 | Outlined in the logo file; never live text |
| **Headings** | Montserrat | 600, 700 | All caps, letterspaced, for section heads |
| **Body** | Open Sans, Source Sans 3 or Lato | 400, 600, 700 | Humanist sans; high legibility at small sizes |
| **Script accent** | A casual script — "Hope. Healing. Horizons." | — | One use only, see below |

### Scale

| Level | Print | Web | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section head | 18–22pt | 28–36px | Montserrat 700, caps, +60 tracking, purple, centred |
| Sub-head | 12–14pt | 18–20px | Montserrat 600 or body 700, ink |
| Body | 10–11pt | 16–17px | Body regular, ink, 1.5–1.6 leading |
| Caption / label | 8–9pt | 13–14px | Body 600, caps for labels |
| Tagline | 13–15pt | 20–24px | Body 700, Tagline Blue |

### Rules

- **All caps only for section heads, pillar labels and short field labels.** Never
  for a sentence, and never for body copy — it cuts reading speed substantially,
  which matters for an audience that includes people with cognitive and
  attention difficulties.
- **Always add tracking to caps.** 40–80 units. Untracked caps are hard to read.
- **Body copy is never below 10pt in print or 16px on screen.**
- **Leading of 1.5 minimum** on body. Generous leading is the cheapest reading-
  comfort intervention available.
- **The script face has exactly one job:** the "Hope. Healing. Horizons." sign-off.
  Never use it for a heading, never for body, never for anything a client must
  read to act. Scripts are low-legibility by nature.
- Left-align body copy. Centre only section heads and short standalone lines.

## 6. Iconography

### Style

The brochure uses **outlined line icons** with rounded terminals, each in a
different palette hue, roughly 1.5–2px stroke at 24px.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Style | Outline / line, not filled, not duotone |
| Stroke | 1.5–2px at 24px, scaling proportionally |
| Terminals | Round cap, round join |
| Grid | 24 × 24 |
| Colour | One palette hue per icon; vary across a list |

### Rules

- **Icons are decorative when a text label sits beside them.** Mark them
  `aria-hidden="true"` on the web. An icon next to "Medication Education" adds
  nothing for a screen reader but noise.
- **Never use an icon as the only indicator of meaning.** Always pair with text.
- **Do not mix icon styles.** One line-icon set throughout. Do not import filled
  or 3D icons from a different library.
- **Vary hue across a list, but keep it below the pillar colours in hierarchy.**
  Service-list icons may cycle through the palette; the pillar icons are fixed.

### Two special icon treatments

- **Check marks** — solid purple circle, white check. Used for benefit lists
  ("Why Choose Us", "Scan To"). Consistent and always purple.
- **Contact icons** — solid purple circle, white glyph. Used for address, hours,
  phone, fax, email, website.

## 7. Graphic devices

### 7.1 The rainbow wave

A flowing multi-colour band — purple → blue → green → orange — anchoring the foot
of a layout.

- **Bottom edge only.** It is a horizon line; it does not belong at the top.
- **Full bleed.** It runs off both edges.
- **Never behind body text.** Headline or wordmark only, and only in white.
- **One per surface.** Two waves on one page is a pattern, not an anchor.

### 7.2 The leaf ornament

A small green sprout sits below each section head, centred, above or on a thin
rule. It ties the section heads to the tree in the emblem.

- Always centred under the head, never inline.
- Always green, never recoloured to match the section.
- Optional at small sizes — drop it before you shrink it below legibility.

### 7.3 Section head rule

Section heads carry a thin horizontal rule beneath, in a light tint of the
purple, interrupted at centre by the leaf. Keep the rule the full width of the
column, not the width of the text.

### 7.4 Panels

| Panel | Ground | Text | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Highlight | Lavender `#F0EBF7` | Ink + purple heads | Grouped supporting content |
| Utility | Cool Mist `#EAF0F2` | Ink + purple heads | QR codes, secondary actions |
| Statement | Navy `#1B3A6B` | White | One key fact — service area, hours |
| Gradient | Purple → blue → teal | White | Quotes over photography |

Corner radius: 8–12px at brochure scale. Consistent across all panels.

## 8. Layout and composition

### 8.1 The three-register structure

The brochure's panel logic is the brand's layout system, and it maps directly
onto a web page:

```
┌──────────────┬──────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│  IDENTITY    │   SUBSTANCE      │   REASSURANCE   │
│              │                  │                 │
│  Logo        │  Our Services    │  Why Choose Us  │
│  Pillars     │  Who We Serve    │  Contact        │
│  Tagline     │  Referral paths  │  QR / next step │
│  Mission     │  Service area    │                 │
│  Photograph  │                  │                 │
│  Quote       │                  │                 │
└──────────────┴──────────────────┴─────────────────┘
   emotional        factual            closing
```

Left establishes who, centre proves what, right removes doubt and gives the
next step. On the web this becomes vertical sections in the same order.

### 8.2 Grid and spacing

- **Print:** 3-column tri-fold, 3mm minimum internal gutter, 5mm safe margin
  from any trim edge.
- **Web:** 8px spacing base. Section padding 64–112px, fluid.
- **Lists:** hairline rules between items, as in "Our Services". Rules in a light
  neutral or light purple tint — never a saturated hue.

### 8.3 Composition rules

- **One section head per block, always centred, always with the rule and leaf.**
- **Body copy left-aligned**, maximum 65–70 characters per line.
- **Panels group, they do not decorate.** Only use a tinted panel when the content
  inside genuinely belongs together.
- **Every surface ends with a next step.** The brochure ends with contact details
  and a QR code. A web page ends with a CTA and the phone number.

## 9. Voice and tone

### 9.1 The two registers

**To referrers:** precise, credentialed, logistical. They need to know scope,
catchment, eligibility and how fast you respond.

> "We serve children, adolescents, and adults receiving behavioral health
> services who would benefit from Psychiatric Rehabilitation Program services."

**To clients and families:** plain, warm, concrete, dignifying.

> "Our program helps individuals build life skills, increase independence,
> strengthen coping abilities, and achieve meaningful recovery goals."

### 9.2 Principles

- **Lead with capability, not deficit.** "Build independence", not "address
  deficits". PRP is rehabilitation — the subject of every sentence should be a
  person doing something.
- **Person-first language, always.** "Individuals receiving services", not "the
  mentally ill". "A person with schizophrenia", not "a schizophrenic".
- **Say what actually happens.** Referrers and families both want specifics.
- **Never promise outcomes.** Describe what the programme provides; do not
  guarantee recovery, and never imply a timeline.
- **Short sentences, ordinary words.** Part of the audience has cognitive,
  attention or literacy difficulties, and families are often reading under stress.

### 9.3 Vocabulary

| Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|
| individuals, participants, people we serve | patients, cases, consumers, the mentally ill |
| build, strengthen, develop, achieve | fix, cure, treat (for PRP specifically) |
| skills, independence, community integration | compliance, management |
| person-centered | client-focused (vaguer, means less) |
| Psychiatric Rehabilitation Program (PRP) | RRP — **see §12.1, this is wrong in the current brochure** |

### 9.4 The three taglines

The brand carries three, each with a distinct job. Do not use more than one per
surface.

| Line | Register | Where |
|---|---|---|
| "Strong Minds. Brighter Futures. Better Lives." | Primary positioning | Under the logo; homepage hero |
| "Empowering people today for a brighter tomorrow." | Emotional, quotable | Over photography; social |
| "Hope. Healing. Horizons." | Sign-off, script | Foot of a layout only |

## 10. Photography

The brochure's photograph — five people seen from behind, arms around each other,
walking into open parkland in warm light — is exactly right, and worth
articulating so the next one matches.

### What makes it work

- **Backs to camera.** Nobody is identifiable, which sidesteps the consent and
  dignity problem entirely.
- **Connection is the subject.** Arms around shoulders — the content is support,
  not treatment.
- **Forward-facing, open ground, warm low light.** It is literally a horizon.
- **Real, mixed, unposed.** Range of ages and ethnicities without looking like a
  diversity stock set.

### Direction

- Warm, natural, golden-hour light. Never fluorescent, never clinical.
- Outdoors, or bright domestic and community interiors.
- People **doing** things: walking, cooking, working, learning, talking.
- Mixed ages and backgrounds, unposed.

### Never

- Identifiable clients — **even with consent**, given the stigma attached to
  behavioral health.
- Anyone in a clinical or institutional setting: hospital beds, waiting rooms,
  medication trays.
- Isolation tropes: a person alone in shadow, head in hands, rain on glass.
- Anything that could be read as depicting a real person's diagnosis.

## 11. Accessibility

**Target: WCAG 2.2 Level AA** for all digital work.

This audience makes accessibility more consequential than usual: it includes
people with cognitive, attention, sensory and literacy difficulties, and families
reading under acute stress.

### Non-negotiables

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Contrast | Body text ≥ 4.5:1, large text and UI ≥ 3:1. **Orange and green fail — see §4.3.** |
| Text size | ≥16px web, ≥10pt print |
| Line length | 65–70 characters maximum |
| Leading | ≥1.5 on body |
| Caps | Section heads and labels only, never sentences |
| Colour independence | Never carry meaning by hue alone — pillar colours always sit with their labels |
| Keyboard | Everything reachable and operable; visible focus, never removed |
| Alt text | Meaningful, or `alt=""` when decorative |
| Motion | Honour `prefers-reduced-motion`; nothing auto-plays |
| Forms | Real labels, clear errors that say what to do |
| Plain language | Aim for a grade 6–8 reading level on client-facing copy |

### Print-specific

- Minimum 10pt body. Offer a **large-print version at 16pt** — say so on the
  brochure and on the site.
- Never set body copy over a photograph or a gradient.
- Never rely on colour alone in a table or diagram.
- Keep the QR code ≥ 20mm and always pair it with the plain URL — QR codes are
  unusable to anyone without a working smartphone camera.

## 12. Compliance and content rules

### 12.1 Fix this before the next print run

> **The brochure says "Psychiatric Rehabilitation Program (RRP)" on the left
> panel and "(PRP)" in the centre panel. PRP is correct.** "RRP" is not a
> recognised Maryland service category, and referrers — who are the primary
> audience for this brochure — will notice. Correct it everywhere.

### 12.2 Other items to resolve

| Item | Issue | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| `healinghorizonsbh@gmail.com` | A consumer mailbox on a healthcare brochure reads as provisional, and a free Gmail account cannot be covered by a Business Associate Agreement | Move to `info@healinghorizonsbh.com` on the domain you already own, with a BAA-covered provider |
| Phone (443) 123-4567 / Fax -4568 | Sequential digits — these look like placeholders | Confirm before printing |
| Hours: Tuesday–Friday | Four days; referrers will ask about Monday | Confirm, and state after-hours guidance |
| No crisis line | A behavioral health surface with no crisis number is a real gap | **Add 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline** to every surface — see below |
| No licence / accreditation numbers | Referrers look for these | Add Maryland licence and any COMAR/CARF accreditation |
| No NPI | Required for billing conversations | Add |

### 12.3 Crisis information is mandatory

**Every client-facing surface must carry crisis resources**, and the current
brochure carries none. At minimum:

> **In a crisis:** Call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) · Call **911**
> if there is immediate danger.

State plainly that the organisation is **not a crisis service** and does not
monitor messages after hours.

### 12.4 Testimonials

**Do not publish client testimonials.** Behavioral health clients are, by
definition, a population vulnerable to undue influence, and soliciting
testimonials from them raises both ethics-board and HIPAA exposure — a
testimonial inherently discloses that its author received services.

Use instead: referrer statements (with written permission), verifiable facts
(years operating, staff credentials, counties served, response time), and
accreditations.

### 12.5 Claims

Never guarantee outcomes. Never state or imply a recovery timeline. Never publish
a statistic without a source. Never publish a licence or credential that is not
current and verifiable.

## 15. Reconciling with the existing website

The site in this repository is built to `DESIGN-GUIDE.md` — the singular
"Healing Horizon", Portland, teal palette. This brochure is a different
organisation. Three honest options:

**1. Rebrand the site to this guide.** The right call if the brochure is the real
business. The work is contained because the site is fully tokenised: the palette
lives in `src/styles/tokens.css` and no component holds a hex value, so the
colour swap is one file. Beyond that: replace `src/data/site.js`,
`src/data/services.js` and `src/data/team.js` with the PRP services and Maryland
details, add the pillar row, add the crisis block, and swap the logo. Roughly a
day's work, not a rebuild.

**2. Keep them separate.** Valid if these are genuinely two entities.

**3. Treat this as a print-only sub-brand.** Not recommended — two visual systems
under one name confuses referrers, who are the audience least tolerant of
ambiguity.

**My recommendation: option 1.** The brochure brand is more developed, more
specific, and describes a real service in a real place. The existing site's
architecture was built to survive exactly this kind of change.
